## 1. Classification Taxonomy

To prevent unjustified novelty claims and ensure strict scientific discipline, every research direction is classified into exactly one of seven standardized categories:

1. **`Already well established`**: Extensively studied in peer-reviewed literature with verified attacks, mathematical models, or published countermeasures.
2. **`Established but our implementation differs`**: The core concept exists in literature, but prior work focused on different targets (e.g., custom FPGA/ASIC hardware or microcontrollers like Cortex-M4 via `pqm4`), whereas our work tests production C90 software (`mlkem-native` / `mldsa-native` / `liboqs`).
3. **`Partially explored`**: Isolated studies exist addressing subsets of the problem (e.g., faulting isolated NTT multipliers), but without end-to-end algorithmic or protocol context.
4. **`Appears underexplored`**: Few or no systematic peer-reviewed publications address this specific question, though adjacent literature exists.
5. **`Unclear — requires more literature`**: Ambiguous boundaries in existing papers; ongoing debate regarding feasibility or formal models.
6. **`Potentially novel but NOT established`**: A promising candidate research gap that lacks direct prior art, but has not yet been rigorously proven experimentally or mathematically in this project.
7. **`Not novel by itself`**: Routine engineering, standard testing, or straightforward parameter re-evaluation that does not constitute a scholarly contribution on its own.

## 3. Critical Reality Check: What We Must NOT Claim

Based on this systematic comparison against the published literature:

1. **DO NOT CLAIM:** *"We are the first to discover that the FO transform comparison is vulnerable to fault injection."*  
   *Refutation:* Pessl & Prokop (TCHES 2021), Hermelink et al. (INDOCRYPT 2021), Delvaux (TCHES 2022), and Jaiswal et al. (TIFS 2024) have extensively analyzed FO comparison faults.
2. **DO NOT CLAIM:** *"We are the first to study fault injection on the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT)."*  
   *Refutation:* Ravi et al. (TCHES 2023), Mozaffari-Kermani et al. (TCAD 2023), and Fritzmann et al. (TCAS-I 2022) have deeply explored NTT fault attacks and error detection.
3. **DO NOT CLAIM:** *"Our software bit-flip experiment proves spacecraft radiation hardness."*  
   *Refutation:* NASA-HDBK-4002A and ECSS-Q-ST-60-15C require beam testing, LET threshold quantification, and microarchitectural modeling. Software memory mutations are an abstract operational proxy, not physical qualification.
4. **DO NOT CLAIM:** *"NTT faults universally cause implicit rejection in ML-KEM."*  
   *Refutation:* Our own Phase 4B validation audit demonstrated that Stage 1 butterfly faults diffuse across 128 coefficients and are completely absorbed by lossy compression rounding ($d_u=10, d_v=4$), resulting in 100.00% legitimate decapsulation.

## 4. Where the Genuine Research Gaps Lie

The intersection of the literature reveals three defensible, high-value candidate gaps:

1. **Gap A: Mathematical Error Diffusion vs. Lossy Compression Quantization Boundaries in Module-Lattice KEMs.**  
   *Why defensible:* Prior fault attacks focused exclusively on adversarial key extraction via large perturbations. The interaction between intermediate butterfly diffusion and ciphertext compression quantization thresholds ($d_u, d_v$) has never been systematically modeled or bounded.
2. **Gap B: End-to-End Cryptographic Fault Propagation into Spacecraft Telecommand Protocols.**  
   *Why defensible:* Space communication literature stops at link budgets and packet fragmentation. Cryptographic literature stops at the algorithm boundary. Connecting internal PQC algorithmic failure modes (implicit rejection vs. candidate mismatch vs. decoding error) to multi-stage telecommand framing, magic sync rejection, and autonomous session recovery bridges two disconnected fields.
3. **Gap C: Cross-Implementation Fault Behavior Across Verified vs. Unverified Production Software.**  
   *Why defensible:* Comparing how CBMC-verified portable C90 (`mlkem-native`) responds to internal faults compared to SIMD-optimized dynamically linked libraries (`liboqs`) provides actionable guidance for high-assurance mission-critical deployment.
